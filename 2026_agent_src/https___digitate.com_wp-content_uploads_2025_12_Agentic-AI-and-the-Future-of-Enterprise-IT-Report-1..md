Introduction:
From Automation to Autonomous

The enterprise AI journey has evolved quickly over the past

three years. In 2023, many organizations focused on

automating routine tasks and reducing operational

complexity. By 2024, the focus had broadened to the

integration of AI into business and IT workflows, though

maturity and governance varied significantly.

By 2025, in North America, adoption has accelerated and

expectations have shifted. Organizations are not only

implementing AI; they are working to scale it, standardize it,

and extract greater strategic value from it. This evolution

reflects a broader shift from automation, where tasks are

streamlined or accelerated, to autonomous IT, where systems

collaborate with human teams to anticipate needs, propose

actions, and support or execute decision pathways.

Purpose of this Report

Digitate’s current research provides an in-depth view of how

Agentic AI is influencing enterprise maturity and operational

performance. It draws on our previous research in 2023

(North America) and 2024 (Europe) to understand how

enterprise priorities, outcomes, and challenges have evolved

over time. The findings presented here are intended to

support IT leadership teams as they plan, scale, and govern

the next phase of AI-enabled transformation.

The survey investigates:

• Adoption and utilization of AI and Agentic AI.

• Current and future levels of AI maturity and trust.

• Obstacles to implementation and value realization.

• How enterprises plan to measure and scale the impact of AI

initiatives.

Definitions

• AI tools: Systems that execute defined, often generative,

functions within set parameters.

• Agentic AI: Broader intelligent systems that reason, adapt,

and act autonomously toward defined goals.

• AI agents: Specific task-oriented implementations of

agentic capabilities within IT or business processes.

Methodology

600 IT decision-makers in the United States and Canada from

organizations with 1,000+ employees that have implemented

AI within the past 24 months, were surveyed online by Sapio

Research (Sept–Oct 2025).

Results are accurate to ± 4% at the 95% confidence level.

1.1 Adoption and Maturity

Adoption of AI is now universal, but the story of maturity is one
of evolution and differentiation.

Every organization surveyed has implemented AI in the last two
years, using an average of five AI tools across multiple
functions. Generative AI remains the most widely deployed
(74%), while over two in five have already introduced agentic
(44%) or agent-based AI (43 %).

The current data highlights a three-year progression that began with
automation in 2023 and evolved through integrated AI and automation
in 2024, reflecting a steady climb from automation as an operational
necessity to AI as a strategic enabler. Indeed, nearly three-quarters
expect to operate at least semi-autonomously by 2030.

In 2023, enterprises were primarily focused on streamlining processes
and reducing manual overhead. By 2024, European organizations were
experimenting with generative and predictive models while prioritizing
data governance. By 2025, North American enterprises have entered
the Agentic AI stage, characterized by value realization and growing
autonomy. ROI has been consistent across regions, and adoption has
become embedded in core IT and business operations.

This cross-year evolution underscores a broader pattern: as

automation matures, organizations transition from reactive,

tactical adoption, to proactive, strategic operations, laying the

groundwork for the fully autonomous enterprise.

• C-suite leaders express higher trust, with 61% saying AI is

sees AI as a path to long-term transformation, while practitioners

focus on pragmatic delivery and governance. Both perspectives

are essential. The trust dynamic within organizations highlights

the importance of collaboration between those setting AI strategy

and those implementing it.

IT as the Proving Ground for AI

2.1 Where AI Delivers the Most Value

Across three years of Digitate’s research, one constant is clear:
IT is both the testing ground and the proving ground for
enterprise AI.

In 2025, that position has only strengthened. AI deployments
are most mature within IT operations, where complexity, data
richness, and the demand for precision create ideal conditions
for autonomous intelligence.

More than three-quarters (78%) of respondents have deployed AI
in IT operations —the highest rate of any business function.
Planned deployments (70%)  remain equally strong, while 65% of
organizations cite ITOps as the function deriving the most benefit
from AI —indicating that IT continues to be the foundation upon
which AI capabilities are scaled across the enterprise.

The top areas of implementation include:
• Cloud visibility and cost optimization (52%) - using AI to

interpret vast telemetry data and anomaly detection in real-
time, as well as providing a single, unified view of spending data
across multi-cloud and hybrid environments.

The Rise of Agentic AI
3.1 From Automation to Collaboration

If the first two waves of enterprise automation and AI adoption
centered on accuracy, efficiency and cost reduction, the next
phase of Agentic AI is changing this paradigm from automation
to collaboration.

Agentic systems can interpret dynamic conditions, reason through
ambiguity, and engage in goal-oriented workflows. Instead of
executing predefined tasks, they are able to collaborate, both with
systems and with people, to deliver outcomes.

In this model, the role of humans also evolves. IT and business
leaders are shifting from managing systems to orchestrating
collaboration between humans and AI agents, where agents act as
digital teammates: proactive, context-aware, and continuously
learning from feedback loops.

3.2 Expectations and Early Adoption

The data shows that enterprises are at an inflection point in
their AI evolution:

• 62% expect Agentic AI to assist in new or previously

unsupported functions.

• 54% expect AI agents to act as workflow or personal

assistants, working alongside humans and helping to

automate contextual, day-to-day activities.

• 53% envisage Agentic AI as enabling full

departmental automation.

• 52% believe Agentic AI will reshape essential tasks

within their roles.

Cost–Human Conundrum

As AI becomes embedded across enterprise operations,
organizations face an increasingly complex equation:
how to scale AI affordably while maintaining the human
capability needed to sustain it.

Digitate’s latest findings expose a persistent dilemma: AI is
deployed to reduce human workload and operational cost, yet
those very same forces remain the primary barriers to its growth.

This “cost–human conundrum” defines the next challenge for AI
maturity - balancing automation’s promise with the realities of
economics, skills, and oversight.

Organizations identify a mix of external pressures and internal
structural risks shaping their AI strategy.

External threats are dominated by financial and security pressures:

• Cybersecurity is cited by 49% of respondents as the most

significant external risk.

• The rising cost of technology ranks second (42%), reflecting

inflationary pressures on infrastructure, cloud compute, and AI

model training.

• Macroeconomic concerns come third (36%), as organizations

prepare to manage risks like recession or inflation.

Crucially, AI is also seen as a means to mitigate several of
these risks.

4.3 The Cost–Human Conundrum Explained

Enterprises are simultaneously navigating three
intersecting pressures:

Enterprises are simultaneously navigating three

intersecting pressures:

The human factor: Skilled professionals are

indispensable for developing, monitoring, and

governing AI. Demand for this talent exceeds supply.

The financial factor: As AI sophistication grows, so too

do its cost. Computing power, data management, and

compliance investments all rise in tandem.

The strategic factor: Leadership expects AI to deliver

measurable ROI and cost efficiency, creating near-

term pressure for rapid payback.

The result is a self-reinforcing loop:

AI investment increases operational capacity but also

raises human and financial dependencies.

Organizations must therefore approach AI not as a

static tool but as an evolving ecosystem requiring

governance, workforce strategy, and financial foresight.

This tension underscores the maturity challenge of 2025 and beyond: enterprises are not questioning if AI delivers

value, but rather how to sustain that value amid escalating costs and limited human capacity. The winners will be

those that manage to balance automation with augmentation - optimizing cost without eroding the human expertise

that powers AI’s success.

Conclusion

Over three years of research, Digitate has tracked a
clear progression: from automation for efficiency to AI
for intelligence, and now to Agentic AI for enterprise
value creation.

What began as a cost-saving measure has evolved into a
strategic driver of business growth, resilience, and innovation.

By now, enterprise AI is no longer an experimental
technology: it is a trusted operational capability delivering
measurable financial impact. Organizations report both
higher ROI and greater confidence in AI’s ability to support
decision-making, governance, and performance. Yet, as this
report shows, the enterprise challenge has shifted. Success is
no longer defined by adoption alone, but by the ability to
scale AI sustainably, balancing automation with human
judgment and cost with capability.

Agentic AI represents the next inflection point.

These intelligent, goal-oriented systems are enabling
enterprises to move from reactive operations to proactive,
autonomous ecosystems, where IT acts as a strategic
orchestrator and ROI enabler. The rise of Agentic AI and AI
agents, working in tandem with human teams, signals a
future in which organizations can anticipate change, adapt
dynamically, and unlock new dimensions of business value.

The road to the autonomous enterprise will require continued
investment in trust, transparency, and talent. Governance
frameworks must evolve from policy to practice, ensuring
that every AI-driven decision is explainable and aligned to
enterprise ethics. Workforce strategies must focus on
upskilling and hybrid expertise, preparing humans and
machines to collaborate seamlessly.

Enterprises that succeed will be those that view AI not as a
