Autonomous artificial intelligence agent

In the context of [generative artificial intelligence](/wiki/Generative_artificial_intelligence "Generative artificial intelligence"), **AI agents** (also referred to as **compound AI systems** or **agentic AI**) are a class of [intelligent agents](/wiki/Intelligent_agent "Intelligent agent") that can pursue goals, use tools, and take actions with varying degrees of autonomy. In practice, they usually operate within human-defined objectives, constraints, and available tools.[[1]](#cite_note-NIST-AI-agent-standards-1)[[2]](#cite_note-IBM-AI-agents-2)

## Overview

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=AI_agent&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Overview")]

AI agents possess several key attributes, including goal-directed behavior, natural language interfaces, the capacity to use external tools, and the ability to perform multi-step tasks. Their control flow is frequently driven by [large language models](/wiki/Large_language_models "Large language models") (LLMs). Agent systems may also include memory components, planning logic, tool interfaces, and [orchestration software](/wiki/Orchestration_(computing) "Orchestration (computing)") for coordinating agent components.[[2]](#cite_note-IBM-AI-agents-2)[[3]](#cite_note-GoogleCloud-AI-agents-3)

AI agents do not have a standard definition.[[4]](#cite_note-4)[[5]](#cite_note-5)[[6]](#cite_note-6) NIST has described agentic AI as an emerging area requiring standards for secure operation, interoperability, and reliable interaction with external systems.[[1]](#cite_note-NIST-AI-agent-standards-1)

A common application of AI agents is the automation of tasks, for example booking travel plans based on a user's [prompted request](/wiki/Prompt_engineering "Prompt engineering").[[7]](#cite_note-The_National_Law_Review-2024-7)[[8]](#cite_note-World_Economic_Forum-2024-8)[[9]](#cite_note-Knight-2024-9)

Companies such as [Google](/wiki/Google "Google"), [Microsoft](/wiki/Microsoft "Microsoft") and [Amazon Web Services](/wiki/Amazon_Web_Services "Amazon Web Services") have offered platforms for [deploying](/wiki/Software_deployment "Software deployment") pre-built AI agents.[[10]](#cite_note-10) Several protocols have been proposed for standardizing inter-agent communication, with examples including the [Model Context Protocol](/wiki/Model_Context_Protocol "Model Context Protocol"), [Gibberlink](/wiki/Gibberlink "Gibberlink"),[[11]](#cite_note-11) and many others. Some of these protocols are also used for connecting agents with external applications.[[12]](#cite_note-Holmes-2025a-12)

In December 2025, [Linux Foundation](/wiki/Linux_Foundation "Linux Foundation") announced the formation of the [Agentic AI Foundation](/wiki/Agentic_AI_Foundation "Agentic AI Foundation") (AAIF), with the goal of ensuring agentic AI evolves transparently and collaboratively.[[13]](#cite_note-13)[[14]](#cite_note-14)

## History

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=AI_agent&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: History")]

Main article: [History of artificial intelligence](/wiki/History_of_artificial_intelligence "History of artificial intelligence")

AI agents have been traced back to research from the 1990s, with Harvard professor [Milind Tambe](/wiki/Milind_Tambe "Milind Tambe") noting that the definition of an AI agent was not clear at the time either. Researcher [Andrew Ng](/wiki/Andrew_Ng "Andrew Ng") has been credited with spreading the term "agentic" to a wider audience in 2024.[[15]](#cite_note-15)

## Training and testing

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=AI_agent&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Training and testing")]

Researchers have attempted to build [world models](/wiki/Foundation_model#World_models "Foundation model")[[16]](#cite_note-16)[[17]](#cite_note-17) and [reinforcement learning](/wiki/Reinforcement_learning "Reinforcement learning") environments[[18]](#cite_note-18) to train or evaluate AI agents. For example, video games such as [Minecraft](/wiki/Minecraft "Minecraft")[[19]](#cite_note-19) and [No Man's Sky](/wiki/No_Man%27s_Sky "No Man's Sky")[[20]](#cite_note-20) as well as replicas of company websites,[[21]](#cite_note-21) have also been used for training AI agents.

## Autonomous capabilities

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=AI_agent&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Autonomous capabilities")]

The *[Financial Times](/wiki/Financial_Times "Financial Times")* compared the autonomy of AI agents to the [SAE classification of self-driving cars](/wiki/Self-driving_car#SAE_classification "Self-driving car"), comparing most applications to level 2 or level 3, with some achieving level 4 in highly specialized circumstances, and level 5 being theoretical.[[22]](#cite_note-22)

## Cognitive architecture

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=AI_agent&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Cognitive architecture")]

See also: [Large language model § Agency](/wiki/Large_language_model#Agency "Large language model")

The following are some possible internal design options for reasoning within an agent:[[23]](#cite_note-23)

* [Retrieval-augmented generation](/wiki/Retrieval-augmented_generation "Retrieval-augmented generation")
* ReAct (Reason + Act) pattern is an iterative process in which an AI agent alternates between reasoning and taking actions, receives observations from the environment or external tools, and integrates these observations into subsequent reasoning steps.[[24]](#cite_note-24)
* Reflexion, which uses an LLM to create feedback on the agent's plan of action and stores that feedback in a memory cache.
* A tool/agent registry, for organizing software functions or other agents that the agent can use.
* One-shot model querying, which queries the model once to create the plan of action.

### Reference architecture

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=AI_agent&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: Reference architecture")]

[Ken Huang](/w/index.php?title=Ken_Huang&action=edit&redlink=1 "Ken Huang (page does not exist)") proposed an AI Agent reference architecture, which consists of seven interconnected layers, with each layer building on the functionality of the layers beneath it[[25]](#cite_note-25):

* **Layer 1**: [Foundation models](/wiki/Foundation_model "Foundation model") - provide the core AI engines to power agent capabilities.
* **Layer 2**: Data operations - manage the complex data infrastructure required for AI agent operations, including [Vector database](/wiki/Vector_database "Vector database"), data loaders, [RAG](/wiki/Retrieval-augmented_generation "Retrieval-augmented generation").
* **Layer 3**: Agent frameworks - sophisticated software and tools that simplify the development and management of the AI agents.
* **Layer 4**: Deployment and infrastructure - provide the robust technical foundation for running AI agents.
* **Layer 5**: Evaluation and observability - focus on assessing the safety and performance of AI agents.
* **Layer 6**: Security and compliance - a crucial protective framework ensuring AI agents operate safely, securely, and conform to regulatory boundaries. At this layer security and compliance features embedded into all the AI agent stack layers are integrated together.
* **Layer 7**: Agent ecosystem - represents the AI agents' interface with real-world applications and users.

## Orchestration patterns

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=AI_agent&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Orchestration patterns")]

To execute complex tasks, autonomous agents are often integrated with other agents or specialized tools. These configurations, known as orchestration patterns or workflows, include the following:[[26]](#cite_note-26)[[27]](#cite_note-27)

* **Prompt chaining**: A sequence where the output of one step serves as the input for the next.
* **Routing**: The classification of an input to direct it to a specialized downstream task or tool.
* **Parallelization**: The simultaneous execution of multiple tasks.
* **Sequential processing**: A fixed, linear progression of tasks through a predefined pipeline.
* **Planner-critic**: An iterative pattern where one agent generates a proposal and another evaluates it to provide feedback for refinement.

## Multimodal AI agents

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=AI_agent&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Multimodal AI agents")]

In addition to large language models (LLMs), [vision-language models](/wiki/Vision-language_model "Vision-language model") (VLMs) and [multimodal](/wiki/Multimodal_learning "Multimodal learning") [foundation models](/wiki/Foundation_model "Foundation model") can be used as the basis for agents. In September 2024, [Allen Institute for AI](/wiki/Allen_Institute_for_AI "Allen Institute for AI") released an open-source vision-language model.[[28]](#cite_note-28) [Nvidia](/wiki/Nvidia "Nvidia") released a framework for developers to use VLMs, LLMs and [retrieval-augmented generation](/wiki/Retrieval_augmented_generation "Retrieval augmented generation") for building AI agents that can analyze images and [videos](/wiki/Video_content_analysis "Video content analysis"), including [video search](/wiki/Video_search_engine "Video search engine") and [video summarization](/wiki/Automatic_summarization "Automatic summarization").[[29]](#cite_note-29)[[30]](#cite_note-30) Microsoft released a multimodal agent model – trained on images, video, software [user interface](/wiki/User_interface "User interface") interactions, and [robotics](/wiki/Robotics "Robotics") data – that the company claimed can manipulate [software](/wiki/Software_agent "Software agent") and [robots](/wiki/Robot "Robot").[[31]](#cite_note-31)

## Applications

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=AI_agent&action=edit&section=9 "Edit section: Applications")]

See also: [List of open-source agentic AI handlers](/wiki/Lists_of_open-source_artificial_intelligence_software#Agentic_AI "Lists of open-source artificial intelligence software")

As of April 2025, per the [Associated Press](/wiki/Associated_Press "Associated Press"), there are few real-world applications of AI agents.[[32]](#cite_note-32) As of June 2025, per [*Fortune*](/wiki/Fortune_(magazine) "Fortune (magazine)"), many companies are primarily experimenting with AI agents.[[33]](#cite_note-Goldman-2025-33)

*[The Information](/wiki/The_Information_(website) "The Information (website)")* divided AI agents into seven archetypes: business-task agents, for acting within enterprise software; conversational agents, which act as [chatbots](/wiki/Chatbot "Chatbot") for customer support; research agents, for querying and analyzing information (such as OpenAI Deep Research); analytics agents, for analyzing data to create reports; [software developer or coding agents](/wiki/AI-assisted_software_development "AI-assisted software development") (such as [Cursor](/wiki/Cursor_(code_editor) "Cursor (code editor)")); domain-specific agents, which include specific subject matter knowledge; and [web browser agents](/wiki/AI_browser "AI browser") (such as OpenAI Operator).[[12]](#cite_note-Holmes-2025a-12)

By mid-2025, AI agents have been used in [video game development](/wiki/Video_game_development "Video game development"),[[34]](#cite_note-34) [gambling](/wiki/Gambling "Gambling") (including [sports betting](/wiki/Sports_betting "Sports betting")),[[35]](#cite_note-Knibbs-2025-35) [cryptocurrency wallets](/wiki/Cryptocurrency_wallet "Cryptocurrency wallet")[[35]](#cite_note-Knibbs-2025-35) (including [cryptocurrency](/wiki/Cryptocurrency "Cryptocurrency") trading and [meme coins](/wiki/Meme_coin "Meme coin")[[36]](#cite_note-Kharif-2025-36)) and [social media](/wiki/Social_media "Social media").[[37]](#cite_note-37) In August 2025, *[New York Magazine](/wiki/New_York_(magazine) "New York (magazine)")* described [software development](/wiki/Software_development "Software development") as the most definitive use case of AI agents.[[38]](#cite_note-Herrman-2025-38) Likewise, by October 2025, noting a decline in expectations, *The Information* noted AI coding agents and [customer support](/wiki/Customer_support "Customer support") as the primary use cases by businesses.[[39]](#cite_note-Holmes-2025b-39)

In November 2025, *The Wall Street Journal* reported that few companies that deployed AI agents have received a return on investment.[[40]](#cite_note-40)

### Applications in government

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=AI_agent&action=edit&section=10 "Edit section: Applications in government")]

Several government bodies in the United States and United Kingdom have deployed or announced the deployment of agents, at the local and national level. The city of [Kyle, Texas](/wiki/Kyle,_Texas "Kyle, Texas") deployed an AI agent from Salesforce in March 2025 for [311](/wiki/311_(telephone_number) "311 (telephone number)") customer service.[[41]](#cite_note-41) In November 2025, the [Internal Revenue Service](/wiki/Internal_Revenue_Service "Internal Revenue Service") stated that it would use Agentforce, AI agents from [Salesforce](/wiki/Salesforce "Salesforce"), for the Office of Chief Counsel, [Taxpayer Advocate Services](/wiki/Office_of_the_Taxpayer_Advocate "Office of the Taxpayer Advocate") and the [Office of Appeals](/wiki/Independent_Office_of_Appeals "Independent Office of Appeals").[[42]](#cite_note-42) That same month, [Staffordshire Police](/wiki/Staffordshire_Police "Staffordshire Police") announced that they would trial Agentforce agents for handling non-emergency [101](/wiki/Police_101 "Police 101") calls in the United Kingdom starting in 2026.[[43]](#cite_note-43) In December 2025, the Department of Neighborhoods in Detroit, Michigan, in partnership with a local business, deployed a pilot project in two Detroit districts for an AI agent to be used for customer service calls.[[44]](#cite_note-44)

In February 2025, [Thomas Shedd](/wiki/Thomas_Shedd "Thomas Shedd"), the director of the [Technology Transformation Services](/wiki/Technology_Transformation_Services "Technology Transformation Services"), proposed using AI coding agents across the United States federal government.[[45]](#cite_note-45) A recruiter for the [Department of Government Efficiency](/wiki/Department_of_Government_Efficiency "Department of Government Efficiency") proposed in April 2025 to use AI agents to automate the work of about 70,000 United States federal government employees, as part of a startup with funding from [OpenAI](/wiki/OpenAI "OpenAI") and a partnership agreement with [Palantir](/wiki/Palantir_Technologies "Palantir Technologies"). This proposal was criticized by experts for its impracticality, if not impossibility, and the lack of corresponding widespread adoption by businesses.[[46]](#cite_note-46)

In December 2025, the [Food and Drug Administration](/wiki/Food_and_Drug_Administration "Food and Drug Administration") announced that it would offer "agentic AI capabilities" to its staff for "meeting management, pre-market reviews, review validation, post-market surveillance, inspections and compliance and administrative functions."[[47]](#cite_note-47) That same month, the [United States Department of Defense](/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense "United States Department of Defense") launched [GenAI.mil](/wiki/GenAI.mil "GenAI.mil"), an internal platform for American military personnel to use generative AI-based applications based on [Google Gemini](/wiki/Google_Gemini "Google Gemini"), including "intelligent agentic workflows". Defense Secretary [Pete Hegseth](/wiki/Pete_Hegseth "Pete Hegseth") listed applications such as "[conducting] deep research, [formatting] documents and even [analyzing] video or imagery at unprecedented speed."[[48]](#cite_note-48) In December 2025, the [United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement](/wiki/United_States_Immigration_and_Customs_Enforcement "United States Immigration and Customs Enforcement") agency signed a contract with a company for its Enforcement and Removal Operations department to use AI agents for [skip tracing](/wiki/Skiptrace "Skiptrace").[[49]](#cite_note-49)

### Operating systems

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=AI_agent&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Operating systems")]

AI agents have also been integrated into [operating systems](/wiki/Operating_system "Operating system"). Agents have been included in operating systems developed by [Microsoft](/wiki/Microsoft "Microsoft"), [Apple](/wiki/Apple_Inc. "Apple Inc.") and [Google](/wiki/Google "Google").[[50]](#cite_note-The_Economist-2025-50) In November 2025, Microsoft released a test [software build](/wiki/Software_build "Software build") of [Windows 11](/wiki/Windows_11 "Windows 11") that included agents intended to run [background tasks](/wiki/Background_process "Background process"), with the ability to read and write personal files.[[51]](#cite_note-51) In December 2025, ByteDance released Doubao, an AI agent that can be integrated into smartphone operating systems, particularly the Nubia M153 by [ZTE](/wiki/ZTE "ZTE").[[52]](#cite_note-Yang-2025-52) Several apps in China blocked or restricted the agent, citing privacy and security concerns,[[53]](#cite_note-53) including [WeChat](/wiki/WeChat "WeChat"),[[52]](#cite_note-Yang-2025-52) [Alipay](/wiki/Alipay "Alipay"), [Taobao](/wiki/Taobao "Taobao"), [Pinduoduo](/wiki/Pinduoduo "Pinduoduo"), [Ele.me](/wiki/Ele.me "Ele.me"),[[54]](#cite_note-54) and local banks.[[55]](#cite_note-55)

### Web browsing

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=AI_agent&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Web browsing")]

[Web browsers](/wiki/Web_browser "Web browser") with integrated AI agents are sometimes called [agentic browsers](/wiki/AI_browser "AI browser"). Such agents can perform small tedious tasks during web browsing and potentially even perform browser actions on behalf of the user. Products like [OpenAI Operator](/wiki/OpenAI_Operator "OpenAI Operator") and [Perplexity Comet](/wiki/Comet_(browser) "Comet (browser)") integrate a spectrum of AI capabilities including the ability to browse the web, interact with websites and perform actions on behalf of the user.[[56]](#cite_note-56)[[57]](#cite_note-57)

In 2025, Microsoft launched [NLWeb](/wiki/NLWeb "NLWeb"), an agentic web search replacement that would allow websites to use agents to query content from websites by using [RSS](/wiki/RSS "RSS")-like interfaces that allow for the lookup and [semantic retrieval](/wiki/Semantic_search "Semantic search") of content.[[58]](#cite_note-Warren-2025-58) Products integrating agentic web capabilities have been criticised for exfiltrating information about their users to third-party servers and exposing security issues since the way the agents communicate often occur through non-standard protocols.[[58]](#cite_note-Warren-2025-58)

## Proposed benefits

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=AI_agent&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Proposed benefits")]

AI agents have been posited as a means of increasing personal and economic productivity,[[8]](#cite_note-World_Economic_Forum-2024-8)[[59]](#cite_note-Piper-2024-59) fostering greater [innovation](/wiki/Innovation "Innovation"),[[60]](#cite_note-Purdy-2024-60) and liberating users from monotonous tasks.[[60]](#cite_note-Purdy-2024-60)[[61]](#cite_note-Wright-2024-61) However, [Parmy Olson](/wiki/Parmy_Olson "Parmy Olson")'s *[Bloomberg](/wiki/Bloomberg_News "Bloomberg News")* opinion piece argued that agents are best suited for narrow, repetitive tasks with low risk.[[62]](#cite_note-62) Conversely, researchers suggest that agents could be applied to [web accessibility](/wiki/Web_accessibility "Web accessibility") for people with disabilities,[[63]](#cite_note-63) and researchers at Hugging Face propose that agents could be used for coordinating resources such as during [disaster response](/wiki/Disaster_response "Disaster response").[[64]](#cite_note-Mitchell-2025-64) The R&D Advisory Team of the [BBC](/wiki/BBC "BBC") views AI agents as being most useful when their assigned goal is uncertain.[[65]](#cite_note-BBC_Online-2025a-65) [Erik Brynjolfsson](/wiki/Erik_Brynjolfsson "Erik Brynjolfsson") suggests that AI agents are more valuable in enhancing, rather than replacing, humans.[[66]](#cite_note-66)

## Concerns

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=AI_agent&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Concerns")]

Concerns include potential issues of liability,[[59]](#cite_note-Piper-2024-59)[[65]](#cite_note-BBC_Online-2025a-65) an increased risk of [cybercrime](/wiki/Cybercrime "Cybercrime"),[[7]](#cite_note-The_National_Law_Review-2024-7)[[59]](#cite_note-Piper-2024-59) [ethical challenges](/wiki/Ethics_of_artificial_intelligence "Ethics of artificial intelligence"),[[59]](#cite_note-Piper-2024-59) as well as problems related to [AI safety](/wiki/AI_safety "AI safety")[[59]](#cite_note-Piper-2024-59) and [AI alignment](/wiki/AI_alignment "AI alignment").[[7]](#cite_note-The_National_Law_Review-2024-7)[[61]](#cite_note-Wright-2024-61) Other issues involve [data privacy](/wiki/Information_privacy "Information privacy"),[[7]](#cite_note-The_National_Law_Review-2024-7)[[67]](#cite_note-O'Neill-2024-67) weakened human oversight,[[7]](#cite_note-The_National_Law_Review-2024-7)[[59]](#cite_note-Piper-2024-59)[[64]](#cite_note-Mitchell-2025-64) a lack of guaranteed [repeatability](/wiki/Reproducibility "Reproducibility"),[[65]](#cite_note-BBC_Online-2025a-65) [reward hacking](/wiki/Reward_hacking "Reward hacking"),[[68]](#cite_note-Huckins-2025-68) [algorithmic bias](/wiki/Algorithmic_bias "Algorithmic bias"),[[67]](#cite_note-O'Neill-2024-67)[[69]](#cite_note-Lin-2025-69) compounding software errors,[[7]](#cite_note-The_National_Law_Review-2024-7)[[9]](#cite_note-Knight-2024-9) lack of [explainability](/wiki/Explainable_artificial_intelligence "Explainable artificial intelligence") of agents' decisions,[[7]](#cite_note-The_National_Law_Review-2024-7)[[70]](#cite_note-Zittrain-2024-70) [security vulnerabilities](/wiki/Vulnerability_(computer_security) "Vulnerability (computer security)"),[[7]](#cite_note-The_National_Law_Review-2024-7)[[71]](#cite_note-71) stifling competition,[[50]](#cite_note-The_Economist-2025-50) problems with [underemployment](/wiki/Underemployment "Underemployment"),[[69]](#cite_note-Lin-2025-69) [job displacement](/wiki/Workplace_impact_of_artificial_intelligence "Workplace impact of artificial intelligence"),[[8]](#cite_note-World_Economic_Forum-2024-8)[[69]](#cite_note-Lin-2025-69) [cognitive offloading](/wiki/Cognitive_load#Effects_of_the_internet "Cognitive load"),[[72]](#cite_note-72) and the potential for user [manipulation](/wiki/Manipulation_(psychology) "Manipulation (psychology)"),[[70]](#cite_note-Zittrain-2024-70)[[73]](#cite_note-73) [misinformation](/wiki/Misinformation "Misinformation")[[64]](#cite_note-Mitchell-2025-64) or [malinformation](/wiki/Malinformation "Malinformation").[[64]](#cite_note-Mitchell-2025-64) They may also complicate legal frameworks and [risk assessments](/wiki/Risk_assessment "Risk assessment"), foster [hallucinations](/wiki/Hallucination_(artificial_intelligence) "Hallucination (artificial intelligence)"), hinder countermeasures against rogue agents, and suffer from the lack of standardized evaluation methods.[[61]](#cite_note-Wright-2024-61)[[7]](#cite_note-The_National_Law_Review-2024-7)[[74]](#cite_note-74)

They have also been criticized for being expensive[[7]](#cite_note-The_National_Law_Review-2024-7) and having a negative impact on [internet traffic](/wiki/Internet_traffic "Internet traffic"),[[7]](#cite_note-The_National_Law_Review-2024-7) and potentially on the [environment](/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_artificial_intelligence "Environmental impact of artificial intelligence") due to high energy usage.[[65]](#cite_note-BBC_Online-2025a-65)[[75]](#cite_note-75)[[76]](#cite_note-76) According to an estimation by Nvidia CEO [Jensen Huang](/wiki/Jensen_Huang "Jensen Huang"), AI agents would require 100 times more computing power than LLMs.[[77]](#cite_note-77) There is also the risk of increased concentration of power by political leaders, as AI agents may not question instructions in the same way that humans would.[[68]](#cite_note-Huckins-2025-68)

Journalists have described AI agents as part of a push by [Big Tech](/wiki/Big_Tech "Big Tech") companies to "automate everything".[[78]](#cite_note-78) Several CEOs of those companies have stated in early 2025 that they expect AI agents to eventually "join the workforce".[[79]](#cite_note-Agarwal-79)[[80]](#cite_note-80) However, in a preprint study, [Carnegie Mellon University](/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University "Carnegie Mellon University") researchers tested the behavior of agents in a simulated software company and found that none of the agents could complete a majority of the assigned tasks.[[79]](#cite_note-Agarwal-79) Other researchers had similar findings with Devin AI[[81]](#cite_note-81) and other agents in business settings[[82]](#cite_note-82) and freelance work.[[83]](#cite_note-83)

In June 2025, *CNN* argued that statements by CEOs on the potential replacement of their employees by AI agents were a strategy to "[keep] workers working by making them afraid of losing their jobs."[[84]](#cite_note-84) Tech companies have pressured employees to use generative AI models in their work, including AI coding agents. [Brian Armstrong](/wiki/Brian_Armstrong_(businessman) "Brian Armstrong (businessman)"), the CEO of [Coinbase](/wiki/Coinbase "Coinbase"), fired several employees who did not.[[85]](#cite_note-85)[[86]](#cite_note-86) Some business leaders have replaced some of their employees with agents, but have said that the agents would need more supervision than those employees.[[39]](#cite_note-Holmes-2025b-39)

In October 2025, *Futurism* questioned whether Amazon's previously announced efforts to replace parts of its workforce with generative AI and AI agents could have led to the [October 2025 outage of Amazon Web Services](/wiki/Timeline_of_Amazon_Web_Services#Outages "Timeline of Amazon Web Services").[[87]](#cite_note-87) Large technology companies such as Salesforce, [Klarna](/wiki/Klarna "Klarna") and [IBM](/wiki/IBM "IBM") have announced layoffs in 2025, replacing hundreds of their employees in [human resources](/wiki/Human_resources "Human resources") or [customer service](/wiki/Customer_service "Customer service") with AI agents.[[88]](#cite_note-88)[[89]](#cite_note-:0-89)[[90]](#cite_note-90) However, Klarna needed to rehire several human employees.[[89]](#cite_note-:0-89)

[Yoshua Bengio](/wiki/Yoshua_Bengio "Yoshua Bengio") warned at the 2025 [World Economic Forum](/wiki/World_Economic_Forum "World Economic Forum") that "all of the [catastrophic scenarios](/wiki/Existential_risk_from_artificial_general_intelligence "Existential risk from artificial general intelligence") with [AGI](/wiki/Artificial_general_intelligence "Artificial general intelligence") or [superintelligence](/wiki/Superintelligence "Superintelligence") happen if we have agents".[[91]](#cite_note-91)

Financial‑stability bodies have warned that more complex and autonomous "agentic" AI could become a channel for systemic risk in finance.[[92]](#cite_note-OSFI_20251114-92) They distinguish these systems from other AI because they can pursue goals over many steps, call tools, and carry out tasks with relatively little human intervention. In workshops with regulators, central‑bank officials, and industry specialists, participants highlighted risks both from agentic systems built inside financial institutions and from tools offered by technology firms that can initiate or execute financial actions. In one 2025 forum, 44% of experts surveyed judged autonomous or agentic AI systems to be the most likely current source of [AI‑related systemic risk in finance](/wiki/Timeline_of_artificial_intelligence_risks_in_global_finance "Timeline of artificial intelligence risks in global finance").[[92]](#cite_note-OSFI_20251114-92)

In March 2025, [Scale AI](/wiki/Scale_AI "Scale AI") signed a contract with the [United States Department of Defense](/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Defense "United States Department of Defense") to work with them, in collaboration with [Anduril Industries](/wiki/Anduril_Industries "Anduril Industries") and [Microsoft](/wiki/Microsoft "Microsoft"), to develop and deploy AI agents for the purpose of assisting the military with "operational decision-making".[[93]](#cite_note-93) In July 2025, [Fox Business](/wiki/Fox_Business "Fox Business") reported that the company EdgeRunner AI built an [offline](/wiki/Online_and_offline "Online and offline") agent, [compressed](/wiki/Model_compression "Model compression") and [fine-tuned](/wiki/Fine-tuning_(deep_learning) "Fine-tuning (deep learning)") on military information, with the CEO seeing more common LLMs as "heavily politicized to the left". As of that time, the company model is being used by the [United States Special Operations Command](/wiki/United_States_Special_Operations_Command "United States Special Operations Command") in an overseas deployment.[[94]](#cite_note-94) Researchers have expressed concerns that agents and the large language models they are based on could be biased towards aggressive [foreign policy](/wiki/Foreign_policy "Foreign policy") decisions.[[95]](#cite_note-95)[[96]](#cite_note-96)

Research-focused agents have the risk of [consensus bias](/wiki/False_consensus_effect "False consensus effect") and [coverage bias](/wiki/Coverage_error "Coverage error") due to collecting information available on the public Internet.[[97]](#cite_note-97) [*NY Mag*](/wiki/New_York_(magazine) "New York (magazine)") unfavorably compared the user workflow of agent-based web browsers to [Amazon Alexa](/wiki/Amazon_Alexa "Amazon Alexa"), which was "software talking to software, not humans talking to software pretending to be humans to use software."[[98]](#cite_note-98) The same outlet described web browser agents and computer-use agents as an attempt to "[click-farm](/wiki/Click_farm "Click farm") the entire economy."[[99]](#cite_note-99)

Agents have been linked to the [dead Internet theory](/wiki/Dead_Internet_Theory "Dead Internet Theory") due to their ability to both publish and engage with online content.[[100]](#cite_note-100)

Agents may get stuck in [infinite loops](/wiki/Infinite_loop "Infinite loop").[[101]](#cite_note-Marshall-2025-101)[[102]](#cite_note-102)

Since many inter-agent protocols are being developed by large technology companies, there are concerns that those companies could use these protocols for self-benefit.[[103]](#cite_note-Stokel-Walker-2025-103)

A June 2025 [Gartner](/wiki/Gartner "Gartner") report accused many projects described as agentic AI of being rebrands of previously released products, terming the phenomenon as "agent washing".[[38]](#cite_note-Herrman-2025-38)

Researchers have warned about the impact of providing AI agents access to cryptocurrency and [smart contracts](/wiki/Smart_contract "Smart contract").[[36]](#cite_note-Kharif-2025-36)

During a [vibe coding](/wiki/Vibe_coding "Vibe coding") experiment, a coding agent by [Replit](/wiki/Replit "Replit") deleted a production database during a [code freeze](/wiki/Freeze_(software_engineering) "Freeze (software engineering)"), "[covered] up [bugs](/wiki/Software_bug "Software bug") and [issues](/wiki/Software_project_management#Issue "Software project management") by creating fake data [and] fake reports" and [responded with false information](/wiki/Hallucination_(artificial_intelligence) "Hallucination (artificial intelligence)").[[104]](#cite_note-104)[[105]](#cite_note-105) A user of [Google Antigravity](/wiki/Google_Antigravity "Google Antigravity") reported that, when the user attempted to use the system to delete [cache](/wiki/Cache_(computing) "Cache (computing)"), the system responded by deleting the user's [D](/wiki/Drive_letter_assignment#Common_assignments "Drive letter assignment") [hard drive](/wiki/Hard_disk_drive "Hard disk drive").[[106]](#cite_note-106)

In July 2025, [PauseAI](/wiki/PauseAI "PauseAI") referred [OpenAI](/wiki/OpenAI "OpenAI") to the [Australian Federal Police](/wiki/Australian_Federal_Police "Australian Federal Police"), accusing the company of violating Australian laws through [ChatGPT agent](/wiki/ChatGPT#Agents "ChatGPT") due to the risk of assisting the development of [biological weapons](/wiki/Biological_agent "Biological agent").[[107]](#cite_note-107)

OpenAI co-founder [Andrej Karpathy](/wiki/Andrej_Karpathy "Andrej Karpathy") criticized AI agents as being ineffective and promoting [AI slop](/wiki/AI_slop "AI slop").[[108]](#cite_note-108)

Issues with multi-agent systems include few coordination protocols between component agents, inconsistent performance, and challenges [debugging](/wiki/Debugging "Debugging").[[109]](#cite_note-109)

In November 2025, Anthropic claimed that a group of hackers sponsored by China attempted a [cyberattack](/wiki/Cyberattack "Cyberattack") against at least 30 organizations by using [Claude Code](/wiki/Claude_(language_model) "Claude (language model)") in an agentic workflow, and that several of these infiltrations had succeeded.[[110]](#cite_note-Goodin-2025-110) However, independent cybersecurity researchers questioned the significance of Anthropic's findings.[[110]](#cite_note-Goodin-2025-110)[[111]](#cite_note-111)

Whittaker argued that the push by Big Tech companies to deploy AI agents risked security vulnerabilities across the Internet.[[112]](#cite_note-112)

### Agentic misalignment

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=AI_agent&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Agentic misalignment")]

"Agentic misalignment" refers to situations in which an AI agent's actions or goals diverge from the intentions of its designers. This occurs when an autonomous system pursues unintended strategies to achieve its objectives, a concern studied in AI safety research. Potential examples include AI agents attempting to sabotage an organization's systems when facing updates or deactivation.[[113]](#cite_note-113)

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=AI_agent&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Security")]

### Threat modeling frameworks

[[edit](/w/index.php?title=AI_agent&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Threat modeling frameworks")]

Several frameworks are used to identify and mitigate security risks in agentic AI:[[114]](#cite_note-114)[[115]](#cite_note-115)

* [STRIDE](/wiki/STRIDE_model "STRIDE model"): A Microsoft model that identifies threats across six categories: spoofing, tampering, repudiation, information disclosure, denial of service, and elevation of privilege.
* [MITRE](/wiki/Mitre_Corporation "Mitre Corporation") ATLAS: A knowledge base of adversary tactics and techniques for AI systems.[[116]](#cite_note-116)
* [OWASP](/wiki/OWASP "OWASP") GenAI Security Project: Provides guidance on vulnerabilities associated with generative AI and large language model integration, including guidelines specifically for agentic applications.[[117]](#cite_note-117)
* MAESTRO: A framework from the [Cloud Security Alliance](/wiki/Cloud_Security_Alliance "Cloud Security Alliance") for assessing risks in AI systems throughout their lifecycle.[[118]](#cite_note-118)
