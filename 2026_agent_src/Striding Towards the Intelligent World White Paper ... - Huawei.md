Alex Sinclair
Artificial intelligence is reshaping the direction of the mobile industry and the broader digital
landscape. Intelligent systems are beginning to interact with people, enterprises and their
environments in more dynamic and meaningful ways. This shift presents important opportunities
while also introducing new expectations for the mobile ecosystem. Advanced AI applications,
from next generation smartphone experiences to autonomous systems in transport and urban
infrastructure, will depend on the intelligence, performance and resilience of the mobile network.
Mobile networks were originally designed for human communication, but the intelligent world
ahead requires networks that support continuous interaction with AI agents, real time intelligent
behavior and increasingly complex multimodal services. Meeting these requirements calls for
networks that are more adaptive and capable of handling significantly greater data volumes with
reliable responsiveness. Large uplink capacity, real time performance and deep pervasive coverage
will be essential for delivering AI driven services at scale and ensuring their consistent availability
across industries and society.
This white paper brings together perspectives from across the mobile ecosystem to outline how
wireless networks are evolving in response to these developments. The insights reflect practical
experience and a growing understanding that the progress of AI and the evolution of mobile
networks are closely linked. By examining these trends and requirements, the industry is better
positioned to support the continued growth of intelligent services and to contribute to the
broader digital transformation taking place around the world.
Chief Technology Officer, GSMA

As a multimodal AI system, a VLA model unifies visual perception, language understanding, and action generation,
enabling robots or AI agents to interpret sensory inputs, understand context, and execute actions through
end-to-end learning.
Today, embodied robots typically rely on a device–cloud architecture analogous to a cerebrum–cerebellum split to
balance performance with hardware cost. However, running many tasks on the device side leads to heavy power
consumption. Systems are evolving toward a fast/slow VLA paradigm. Complex perception and
instruction-following (e.g., processing images and language) will require slower, deliberative reasoning, while
behaviors like walking or emergency avoidance will rely on fast, reflex-like processing.
In this device–cloud synergy for embodied intelligence, the network will function as an agile nervous system.
Multimodal perception requires three cameras streaming 30 fps video plus status and sensor data. This will require
uplink speeds of about 20 Mbps. Between the cloud "brain" and the device "cerebellum", control commands must
meet an inference latency on the order of hundreds of ms.
